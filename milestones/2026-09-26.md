# September 26 — programmable nexus and design discussion

Drafted September 17, 2026, updated with ember's programmable-nexus, mixed-application and full-stack sprint instructions. **Status: proposed design under active investigation.** Ember has supplied the release cadence and desired platform shape, explicitly permits a mix of activities, and now authorizes broad autonomous implementation work. The exact runtime, economic mechanism and delivery scope remain open. Authorization to work does not make every design alternative below an accepted decision.

## Planning basis

Releases occur on the 13th and 26th. Ember reports that the nine days to September 26 usually support 27–50 swarmcycles, with ember participating in design and quality work. This is capacity context, not a task estimate. Substantial foundation work is a legitimate candidate. An unresolved integration is work to close; calling it a limitation does not make it permanent.

Ember expects approximately 20–60% of this long sprint may go into the core system and explicitly asks for comprehensive changes up and down the stack. Missing semantic, compiler, proof, persistence or runtime support belongs in the implementation plan. Do not design the milestone around those gaps merely because a weaker route already runs. The active [sprint record](../sprints/2026-09-17.md) will distinguish accepted intent, engineering decisions, implementation bundles and actual evidence.

The immediate design task is to specify the common experience of inhabiting and extending the nexus, then make its necessary pathways implement consistent meanings. A first application can remain undecided while the resource, authority and participation contracts become concrete. Existing code supplies evidence and reusable machinery. Existing defaults do not automatically define the new product.

## Direction supplied by ember

The offering should be DREGG-native, grow to encompass the suite, include some meaningful on-chain Solana/token utility, and allow participants to run their own DREGG nodes and host cells. Clutch belongs in the design investigation. Ember explicitly permits a mix of workroom, social/game, computation or other activities, centered on programmability at the DREGG kernel level / integration with a programmable shell at some level. Selecting one application genre is not a prerequisite.

Consequently the earlier fieldbook example below is one acceptance workload. It does not determine the product's ontology or exclude other capabilities. The proposed September 26 milestone is an extensible resource environment with an exercised economic connection, whose exact initial operations still need selection.

The programming path is now a primary design object: a person or Hermes authors program/rule/command data, acts under explicit authority, and reaches the selected kernel's evaluation and durable effect path. A conventional shell, a script runtime, a graphical surface and an agent may be different entrances to those resources. Their implementation choices remain open; this clarification does not require inventing a new language or making all OS process execution part of the DREGG kernel.

The [programming investigation](../research/programming.md) locates concrete data-defined cell laws and script-to-effect machinery alongside missing native/proof installation and durable consumers. Those findings supply core implementation obligations, not a reduced release promise.

## A candidate experience to react to

Two friends inhabit a persistent, programmable resource space. One creates a shared fieldbook containing a document, a revision-linked quotation, and a small executable tool. They inspect and change the tool's behavior and presentation from inside the space, with real Hermes acting under explicit delegated authority. The other friend follows a shared link, makes a local branch, and changes it while disconnected. They reconnect, inspect any divergence, and combine or deliberately preserve the alternatives. They can close their sessions, restart the host, and return to their work and its history.

The fieldbook is an example workload, not the selected application. The same environment should let a person discover a resource's supported operations, obtain appropriately scoped authority, and create a new composition that another person can use. A Clutch market or public computation can participate through an explicit interface. The intended result is something friends can extend and repeatedly use without an operator repairing state between steps.

The candidate makes these observable promises:

1. **Arrive and return.** Two distinct people can enter the space, find a resource again, and resume it after session and host restarts.
2. **Use and author.** A person can inspect a resource and change at least one meaningful behavior or composition, as well as its content. The changed behavior persists and is what the other person runs.
3. **Collaborate with Hermes.** A real external Hermes invocation changes the selected resource through the chosen authority and commit path. Its durable result is visible through another participant's interface. A request log alone does not satisfy this step.
4. **Link and branch.** A pinned citation identifies the version it presents. A local branch remains editable when disconnected; after reconnect, independent edits are retained or resolved under the declared conflict rule.
5. **Inspect and recover.** People can follow authorship and revision history. Interrupted or refused operations do not appear as committed work, and replay restores the effects and history the interfaces reported as committed.
6. **Participate from an owned node.** At least one resource is served from a participant-operated node and is usable through the shared environment under the declared identity and authority rules. This establishes a particular hosting/transport path, not arbitrary migration of every resource or federation-wide finality.
7. **Exercise a meaningful Solana operation.** A selected economic operation has an actual chain outcome tied to a named resource, job, entitlement or Clutch object. The resource interface distinguishes requested, submitted, accepted and failed outcomes. The operation, mint, cluster and evidence acceptance rule remain to be chosen.

A local branch on the shellserver alone does not establish operation on a disconnected personal device. The chosen local-first milestone must name the actual device/process boundary it crosses.

## Decisions needed to make those promises precise

These are proposed decision topics, not a questionnaire for ember. Engineering investigations should produce concrete alternatives and recommendations; ember's input is most valuable where alternatives change the intended experience.

| Decision | What needs one explicit meaning across implementations |
|---|---|
| Resource and revision identity | Which objects hold content, behavior, presentation and authority; how versions, replicas and forks refer to their origins; what a link follows or pins |
| Authorized change | Which operations humans and Hermes may perform; how delegation, revocation and admission work; which state changes commit together |
| Durable history | Which records are sufficient to recover the resource; what an acknowledgment promises; how interrupted writes and retries are interpreted |
| Sharing and local operation | Live view, pinned reference and independent replica are explicit modes; offline capabilities, conflicts, reconciliation and settlement have specified rules |
| Execution and evidence | Which semantic definition the selected runtime consumes; the exact statement checked by each verifier; which external effects require separate result evidence |
| Hosting and providers | How personal/shared resource authority differs from authority to execute a public job; which resource offers and verdicts make a provider eligible |
| External protocols and assets | Which state Solana or Clutch owns; which DREGG action requests an external operation; what confirms completion and how its observation changes a DREGG resource |

Consistency does not require a universal cell schema, a single global ledger, or identical internal representations. It requires declared relationships and faithful transitions between the representations actually used. Derived views and caches can be separate; a hidden second authority or unrelated authoring history cannot stand in for the promised resource change.

The minidregg/breadstuffs choice belongs here: select the semantic definitions and receiving runtime for the chosen operations, then decide what to retain, adapt or replace along that path. A new proof, artifact or compiler module counts toward that experience when its receiving path consumes it and the claimed relationship is established.

Clutch's current repository instructions maintain a separate Solana protocol and a small economic kernel. Exposing its operations as nexus resources does not decide to merge its code or replace its economic authority with a DREGG record. Participant-operated hosting similarly needs an actual identity/transport/authority contract; displaying a configurable node URL alone does not establish interoperable cell hosting.

The proposed common resource interface should let a person or agent discover supported operations, inspect permitted state and provenance, construct an authorized request, observe its progress/result, and compose it with another resource. Its exact schemas are still design work. minidregg already has a first-order Hyperdocument interface carrier with request-indexed authority; its negotiation explicitly produces no accepted effect or physical completion. That is relevant semantic material, not an existing network API to presume complete.

An additional acceptance exercise should introduce one new authored resource behavior through this interface and make it usable by the other participant. This tests the mechanism by which the nexus grows after September 26.

### Proposed answers from the Astra review

The [contract review](../research/nexus-contract.md) sharpens these topics into five proposed decisions:

1. A resource's identity is independent of its location, renderer and agent session. Exact revision links, follow-current references and independent branches are explicit modes; a composition identifies the behavior and presentation versions it consumes.
2. Humans and Hermes construct the same typed, request-bound operations under current authority. Interface discovery and negotiation prepare a request; the selected semantic family and receiving handler determine whether it takes effect.
3. Each operation has one authoritative accepted patch/history path, with a declared acknowledgment and recovery promise. Multi-resource operations state which legs commit together; physical durability must be established by their actual handler.
4. Offline alternatives retain their provenance. Admission into shared state rechecks the relevant authority and preserves a merge basis or an explicit conflict; a local draft is not silently treated as shared settlement.
5. External operations retain their protocol's authority. Intent, dispatch, observed result and accepted completion are distinct facts. Each family defines the exact evidence for success, whether that is a document edit, computation, custody change or Clutch claim.

One illustrative workload is an authored, deterministic transform over pinned public documents: another participant hosts execution, and its checked output becomes a linked resource. This tests extension, hosting and result consumption while leaving the application genre open. The current Hyperdocument action grammar supports content and links, not arbitrary tool execution. The computation must use a specifically inspected semantic family/consumer or extend the Lean-owned vocabulary and its receiving implementation. A program label or manifest alone is insufficient. No tool, checker or runtime has been selected by this example.

The extension exercise should let a friend publish a new tool through ordinary resource authoring, without a host-code change for each tool. That requires choosing an admitted programming/evaluation mechanism. Authoring a program within that mechanism and introducing a new trusted semantic family are different operations; the first should not accidentally require the second every time.

The [node interface inventory](../research/node-interfaces.md) locates a concrete native client path: signed-turn ingress plus cell and persistent receipt-head readback. Its admission response is distinct from finalized state. How a participant advertises and acquires the authority/obligation to host a particular resource remains a question for the node/custody investigation; those client routes alone do not settle it.

## Public jobs and real stake

Ember's accepted devnet rule is **real $DREGG locked; penalties recorded only**. The custody mechanism and withdrawal/release conditions remain undecided.

A proposed public-compute extension lets a participant publish an eligible job over a public resource revision, have a contributed host execute it, and bring the checked result back into the resource world. If included, its complete path is resource offer → provider eligibility and lock evidence → bound job/input → result/evidence → challenge/adjudication → durable outcome and recorded penalty. Timeout, incorrect result and custody fault need distinct meanings. A recorded penalty must leave the locked monetary balance unchanged.

Provider admission is not a logical prerequisite for friends authoring on operated hosting. Whether the provider journey belongs in September 26 is a product-scope choice, not a finding that it is too much work. If selected, actual custody and its acceptance path become required work; job-field accounting and a STARK identifier do not establish them. The known statement problems must be addressed in the particular assurance path used, rather than carried forward under a stronger product claim.

See [providers](../research/providers.md) for the existing mechanisms. No chain, contract, token custody operation, funding amount or release transaction is selected here.

The term "devnet" needs two separate fields in this design: the DREGG application's experimental stage and the Solana cluster holding the selected asset. Do not infer that real $DREGG exists on a Solana test cluster or treat a test mint as the real token. The present preference for real stake does not itself select or authorize a custody deployment.

## Economic connections to compare

The follow-up source investigations are [Solana utility](../research/solana.md) and [Clutch](../research/clutch.md). These candidates are design alternatives, not accepted scope or deployment readiness claims.

| Connection | User-visible purpose | Existing material and remaining join |
|---|---|---|
| Holder admission | Sponsor access to a bounded hosted resource or community allocation | PoA's beta holding-capability path and the documented holder portal exist; a native nexus grant consumer is not established. Observing holdings does not itself perform an on-chain mutation. |
| Provider bond | Back an advertised public service obligation with the agreed real-lock/recorded-penalty rule | Escrow, lease and adjudication pieces exist separately. The older lock program requires legacy SPL Token while the holder policy pins Token-2022; provider binding, release terms and verdict-to-recorded-penalty integration remain. |
| Resource payment | Fund an execution/storage/service agreement associated with a resource | Native lease/payment and a dated stand-in-token bridge path exist; exact asset identity, custody, result conditions and the receiving resource need a composed path. |
| Clutch claim/settlement | Hold and operate a funded economic claim as part of a programmable resource composition | Realm/market/claim interfaces, generated layouts and browser/Solana paths exist. A chosen real-token Realm, exact source/payoff contract, selected runtime and nexus operation/result adapter remain. |

My current design hypothesis is to attach provider stake to the specific public obligation a provider advertises. Running a personal node and enrolling as a bonded provider then have distinct admission contracts. Clutch can expose its actual economic operations through the resource interface while retaining its own immutable market facts and custody rules. This hypothesis still needs comparison against the release's chosen operations.

The economic Astra review recommends the provider bond as the leading candidate **if a useful public job with a defensible checker or bounded adjudication is selected**. Payment may fit better if friends primarily want to consume operated capacity; Clutch fits when they want a specific immutable claim. A bond's design must include its complete exit: who can release it, its destination, how active obligations affect withdrawal, and bounded recovery. Recorded-only penalties must not silently gain monetary force through an unspecified indefinite lock. A payment proposal must also distinguish buying capacity/time from buying a checked result; a lock/mirror design additionally owes backing and redemption semantics.

For the public-document tool candidate, the following proposed traces make the exercise reviewable. They leave the exact computation, verifier and custody policy open, and are not execution evidence:

| Trace | Required observable result |
|---|---|
| Success | One request binds the admitted tool version, public input revision and checking policy. The eligible host returns evidence; accepted output is published as a linked resource with that provenance. |
| Refusal | A request with invalid authority or incompatible revisions receives a refusal; it creates no accepted job or resource mutation. |
| Timeout | The declared obligation expires under its stated rule; the resource shows noncompletion. Any adjudicated penalty records that verdict without reducing custody principal. |
| Incorrect result | The checker refuses publication as an accepted result; retained evidence can enter the declared challenge/adjudication path. A provider's output hash alone does not establish correctness. |
| Restart/retry | Recovery reconciles the same request, external transaction and resource outcome. Repeating the request does not duplicate a custody action, output publication or reputation event. |

Clutch acceptance should follow the selected capability: issuance/redemption or funding need their own evidence, while reciprocal trading belongs in the requirement only if trading is promised.

Whichever economic connection is selected, the acceptance journey must consume its outcome in the nexus: which resource became available, which obligation became backed, which service was paid, or which claim changed hands. The external transaction and its DREGG observation are causally related events with separate authorities; cross-chain atomicity is not assumed.

## How to organize the cycles

Start by agreeing the nexus's most consequential resource/authority/history decisions and a small set of operations that exercise them. A final choice of workroom, game or other application is not a prerequisite for that design work. Build the first complete journey through the intended substrate early, with explicitly bounded operations and parameters. Deepen that running journey throughout the release interval. This follows the earlier whole-system-first method; a separate application with a promise to port its semantics later does not satisfy it.

Organize parallel work around obligations of the same journey: semantic transitions and artifact consumers; durable authoring; human/Hermes operations; sharing and reconciliation; hosting and recovery; and, if selected, provider jobs. Assign clear file ownership and a named receiving consumer to each task. Decisions affecting more than one lane need a shared contract revision and an affected-consumer list.

Every implementation cycle should return evidence of what became reachable through the selected path, what failed, and which decision or implementation obligation comes next. Integrate and exercise the journey during development. Keep room within the remaining cycles for installation, recovery, adversarial cases and release rehearsal on the actual receiving configuration. Learn the effective cycle cost from the first integrated closures before allocating all 27–50 cycles.

A lane brief should contain: the intended user operation; its accepted contract revision; exact source signatures and receiving consumer; write ownership; prerequisites; the smallest meaningful acceptance checks and build commands; and the state change that would close the task. An implementation can be complete without its deployment being complete; retain both facts. Proof checks and executed scenarios provide different evidence and must both address the actual chosen claim.

Ember should encounter the evolving experience regularly, while design changes remain inexpensive. The useful review is trying to create, change, share and recover something, with focused decisions surfaced from that activity.
